title: OSS vs Commercial at a glance
---

Zerker is **open-core**. Everything you need to self-host, run, and secure
the gateway is OSS — Apache-licensed, no call home, no usage cap. The line is
drawn at *money movement* and *multi-tenant convenience*: **OSS verifies and
captures; <Commercial /> collects, bills, and governs.** See
[The open-core boundary](/concepts/open-core-boundary/) for the reasoning
behind where the line sits.

| Capability | Tier |
|---|---|
| Gateway, catalog, routing & proxy | OSS |
| MCP-native transport | OSS |
| Auth, SSRF guarding, per-tenant credential isolation | OSS |
| x402 payment **gate** (verify an authorization before forwarding) | OSS |
| Raw invocation & payment capture (via the API) | OSS |
| Self-hosting a facilitator (the `/settle` server) | OSS |
| Settlement / collection, fee modes, managed facilitator | <Commercial /> |
| Usage & revenue metering dashboard, usage-based billing | <Commercial /> |
| Multi-tenant control plane (centralized management, governance) | <Commercial /> |

The short version: you can run Zerker, catalog your agents, proxy their
traffic, and **verify** a stablecoin payment before serving a paid call —
entirely self-hosted, for free. The moment you want Zerker to actually
**collect** that payment on-chain, run it across a fleet of tenants from one
control plane, or hand a finance team a billing dashboard, that's the managed
tier.

Pricing for the managed tier is not yet published — see
[Commercial](/commercial/) for the current state.
